The one decision that shapes everything

You are not building one product. You are building two, and they have opposite economics.

The customer-facing half — website, instant quote, booking, tracking portal — is where you win jobs. Every competitor in Denver has a phone number and a contact form; almost none give a real price in two minutes. This is your differentiation, it is cheap to build, and nobody sells you a good version of it.

The back-office half — dispatch, crew app, invoicing, payroll, insurance documents — is where you lose money if it is bad, but it is identical at every moving company in the country. Elromco, SmartMoving and MoveitPro have each spent a decade on it. Building your own costs six figures and buys you nothing a customer can see.

The recommendation

Build the front. Buy the back. Connect them once. Ship your own quote-and-book experience, subscribe to an existing dispatch platform, and pipe booked jobs from one into the other. Revisit building your own back office only if you pass roughly 200 moves a month and the platform is actively holding you back.

Stage 0 — Before you write any code

Validate the pricing model and pick your platform

2–3 weeks · ~$0

The prototype encodes assumptions: a nine-volume-unit-per-mover-hour throughput, a three-hour minimum, $149/$199/$249 crew rates, a $45 metro travel fee. Those numbers drive every quote the software will ever produce. Get them right before they are load-bearing.

  • Pull your last 60 completed jobs. For each, record inventory, crew size, actual hours, final invoice.
  • Run each one through the prototype's estimator. Count how many land inside the quoted range.
  • Tune throughput and the range width until you hit 85–90% accuracy. This is the single highest-value day of work in the whole plan.
  • Demo three dispatch platforms (Elromco, SmartMoving, MoveitPro). Ask each the same question: can I create a job through your API? If the answer is no, cross them off.
  • Confirm QuickBooks integration and per-truck or per-user pricing in writing.

Exit test: you can state your hourly throughput as a number you trust, and you have a signed platform quote.

Stage 1 — Marketing site and instant quote

The half that wins jobs

4–6 weeks · $6k–$14k

Ship the five public pages and the four-step estimator from the prototype as a real site. No customer accounts yet, no live tracking — a lead that arrives with a priced inventory attached is already a step-change over a contact form.

Scope

  • Home, Pricing, Service area, Commercial, Reviews — static pages, real content, fast.
  • The four-step estimator with server-side pricing (never trust a price computed in the browser).
  • Booking writes a lead to your database and creates the job in the dispatch platform by API.
  • Confirmation email and SMS to the customer; instant SMS alert to whoever answers the phone.
  • Analytics on the estimator funnel: where people abandon, which step loses them.

Stage 2 — Customer portal and payments

Cut the “where are they?” phone calls

4–6 weeks · $5k–$12k

Every move generates three to five inbound calls that a portal answers for free: what time, what do I need to do, what will it cost, where is the crew, send me the insurance certificate.

  • Magic-link login — no passwords, no signup friction.
  • Move timeline, customer checklist, document vault (estimate, certificate of insurance, inventory).
  • Card on file via Stripe. Authorize at booking, capture the real hours after the crew closes the job.
  • Digital bill of lading with a signature captured on the crew's phone.
  • Two-way messaging between customer and crew — SMS underneath, threaded in the portal.

Payments warning. Hourly billing means the final amount is unknown at booking. Use a Stripe authorization for the top of the range, then capture the true amount. Get your terms — three-hour minimum, quarter-hour increments, what happens above the range — reviewed by a lawyer before the first card is charged.

Exit test: a move completes and gets paid without a phone call or a paper form.

Stage 3 — Dispatch integration and the crew's phone

Make the bought platform feel like yours

3–5 weeks · $4k–$10k

You are not rebuilding the dispatch board here. You are making the platform you bought talk to the site you built, so nothing is entered twice and the customer sees the truth.

  • Two-way sync: your bookings create jobs; their status changes update your portal.
  • Crew location published to the portal map on move day only, from the platform's own crew app.
  • Automated review request the morning after, with the referral code attached.
  • A thin internal dashboard for the numbers the platform reports badly: estimate accuracy, close rate by lead source, revenue per crew hour.

Exit test: your office team works in one system, not two, and the customer's tracking view is live without anyone updating it by hand.

Stage 4 — Only if the numbers justify it

Build your own back office

4–6 months · $40k+

Do this only when all three are true: you are past roughly 200 moves a month, the platform is demonstrably costing you jobs or hours, and you have a developer on retainer rather than a project budget. Then rebuild dispatch, the crew app, scheduling and payroll on your own stack — the prototype already shows the shape.

Most companies your size never reach this stage, and that is a good outcome, not a failure.

Hiring: what to actually ask for

You do not need an agency and you do not need a technical co-founder. You need one senior full-stack contractor who has shipped a Next.js and Stripe project before, on a fixed-scope engagement per stage.

  • Show them the prototype first. It is worth more than any written spec — it removes ambiguity about what you want, and it will cut their estimate.
  • Hire for one stage at a time. Stage 1 is the audition.
  • Insist on: your GitHub organisation, your Vercel and Stripe accounts, weekly deploys to a staging URL you can click.
  • Red flags: a fixed bid for all four stages sight-unseen, a proposal that starts with dispatch, anyone who wants to build the back office first, or anyone who dismisses the bought platform without pricing the alternative.
